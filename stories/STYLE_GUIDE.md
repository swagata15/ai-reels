# Story-reel style guide (learned from a Zack D. Films short, frame by frame)

Reference: the "drill through a marble vs. through Earth" short (25 s analysed at 2 fps).

## What the reference does, second by second
| Time | Shot | Technique |
|------|------|-----------|
| 0-2 s | Realistic hand holds a marble; a drill bores through it, sparks flying | **Hook with a tangible, human-scale object.** Hand = instant relatability and scale |
| 2-5 s | Same drill, now above Earth in space; Earth shatters with flying debris | **Scale escalation + spectacle** ("...and then through a sphere the size of Earth") |
| 5-8 s | The drilled Earth ring floats in space | Result shown big, camera keeps moving |
| 8-11 s | Bright cyan studio backdrop; marble ring next to Earth ring; **giant red 3D question mark** | **Ask the viewer a question** ("Which of these two would contain more material?") |
| 11-12 s | A realistic hand points at the Earth ring: "It would be this one, right?" | **Voice the viewer's wrong intuition**, then flip it |
| 12-16 s | Rings flatten; yellow 3D height arrows | **Diagram elements built in 3D** (arrows), not flat overlays |
| 16-17.5 s | Two measuring beakers of water on a wooden table | **Everyday analogy as proof** (same volume) |
| 17.5-21 s | Back in space: the Earth ring stretches; red dimension line counts **2,877 → 4,655 → 6,966 → 7,926 miles** | **Numbers live inside the scene** as a measurement, counting up |
| 21-23 s | Red arrows squeeze it: "shaved down incredibly thin"; a hand pinches the paper-thin ring | Hands again for scale |
| 23-25 s | Marble ring with arrows expanding outward | Payoff / reveal |

## The rules we take from it
1. **Open on a real object in a human hand**, then escalate (small → huge, safe → dramatic).
2. **New visual every 1-2 s.** Cuts, scale jumps, or a big camera move. Never a static shot.
3. **Ask the viewer something**, voice the obvious (wrong) answer, then reveal the real one.
4. **Bright, clean, high-clarity look** for explanation shots: simple gradient studio (cyan here),
   space, or a wooden tabletop. Dramatic/dark only for spectacle moments. (Our v1 was dark neon
   all the time; that reads "dashboard", not "story".)
5. **Stylised realism:** realistic hands and props, simple materials, soft key light, no clutter.
6. **3D annotation language:** red arrows, red dimension lines with counting numbers, a big red
   3D question mark, yellow measurement arrows. All lit in the scene, not flat UI.
7. **Everyday analogy** to prove the point (beakers of water, a pinhead, a grain of salt).
8. **Captions:** 1-3 words at a time, bold white sans-serif with a soft shadow, no box, lower
   third (~70-75% down), switching exactly with the voice.
9. **Narration carries everything:** short, conversational sentences; visuals change on its beats.
